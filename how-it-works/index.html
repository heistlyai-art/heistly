File 06 — Method

Seal. Prove. Driver. Delivered.

The desk does not improvise the order. You seal funds into a note, prove you still hold it, and a Driver posts the payment. The app that will do this is not open. The order is already the product.

Four beats

  1. Seal

    Move ETH, USDC, or USDG into the vault. The chain records a deposit. You receive an encrypted note. That note is the only map back to what you sealed.

  2. Prove

    When you spend, a zero-knowledge proof is built where you sit. It shows you hold an unspent note, without naming which one. Heistly’s servers are not where that proof is assembled.

  3. Driver

    The Driver takes the proof and the destination. It pays the gas and posts the transaction. Your wallet does not sign that send, so your address is not in it.

  4. Delivered

    The address you named is paid from the vault. You keep the note’s secret. The public record shows a delivery, not a line from your wallet to theirs.

Honest split

What is public. What stays with you.

Public

A deposit into the vault is a public event. The chain can see assets arrive. An escape or a getaway is also a transaction the chain can see. What it should not see is your address as the sender of that payout.

With you

The note, the secret inside it, and the proof before it is posted. Heistly does not claim that every observer, on every chain, is blind. “No trail” means your wallet is not on the outgoing send.

Safehouse recovery

Bring a burner home.

A one-time address is a useful way to enter and a poor way to be paid. Safehouse recovery is the return path. The burner seals a note. You hold that note. A later getaway pays a wallet you actually keep. The burner is not the address that receives.

Keep the note. Recovery only works if you still have it. The desk is not a lost-and-found for secrets it never stored.
