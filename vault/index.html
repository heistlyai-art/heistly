File 01 — Vault

Seal it. The street does not get the rest.

A vault deposit is the shield. ETH, USDC, or USDG goes in. You come away with an encrypted note. The chain can see that the vault received funds. It cannot see, from that deposit alone, who you will pay on the way out.

What you can seal

Three assets. One note each.

01

ETH

The gas of the home lanes, sealed like everything else. A later getaway does not ask your wallet to spend it in the open.

02

USDC

A dollar note. Useful when the payment should not swing with the market between the seal and the delivery.

03

USDG

The third bill in the vault. Switch can trade it for ETH or USDC without a public wallet in the middle.

The note

What you hold, and what we do not.

The note is the only map from a deposit to a later getaway. It names the asset, the amount, and a secret. That secret stays with you.

On the note

Asset, amount, and a secret only your keys can use. Lose the note and the sealed funds are not a support ticket. Heistly is not built to store a spare.

A plain rule. If a screen ever asks you to paste a seed phrase into Heistly, it is not this desk.

Safehouse

A burner can walk funds in.

A one-time address can make the deposit. Safehouse recovery is the way those funds stop belonging to that address: you hold the note, and a getaway later pays a wallet you keep. The burner is not the receiver.
